# Startup Co-Founder Question Bank

Ask one at a time. Skip questions that are already answered. ★ = must answer before leaving the phase.

---

## No idea yet (run first if the user has no idea)

1. What industries or jobs have you worked in? What was slow, annoying, or expensive there?
2. Which group of customers can you reach easily (network, community, industry)?
3. What do people already come to you for help with?
4. What kind of company do you want: venture-scale, profitable small business, or side project?
5. How much time and money can you put in over the next 6 months?

→ Generate 3–5 candidate ideas. Score each on pain × access to customers × founder fit × market size, and let the user pick one.

---

## 1 · Problem (8)

1. ★ What problem are you solving? One sentence, without mentioning your solution.
2. ★ Who has this problem? List every type of person or company that feels it (don't narrow down yet; that's Phase 2).
3. ★ How do they solve it today (tools, spreadsheets, agencies, people, ignoring it)?
4. ★ How often does it happen, and what does it cost (hours, dollars, risk, revenue lost)?
5. Is this a top-3 priority for the people who have it, or a nice-to-have?
6. ★ How many people with this problem have you talked to? What did they *do* (pay, switch, build a workaround)?
7. Why now? What changed (technology, AI, regulation, behavior) that makes this solvable or urgent today?
8. What would have to be true for this problem to be *not worth solving*?

**Gate:** painful, frequent, and with evidence from real people? If not, provide a 5-conversation interview script (Mom Test: ask about past behavior, not opinions about the idea).

## 2 · ICP: Ideal Customer Profile (10)

The ICP is chosen from the people who have the problem. It decides how we approach everything after it: what v0 looks like, the price point, the sales motion, and the market size.

1. ★ Of everyone who has this problem, who feels it *most* painfully and would pay to fix it first? Why them?
2. ★ Describe one specific first customer: the person and their company.
3. ★ Is it B2B or B2C? For B2B: industry, company size (employees or revenue), geography, tech maturity.
4. ★ Who is the **user**, who is the **buyer**, and who **approves** the purchase?
5. ★ How does this ICP change the approach? (e.g. an enterprise ICP means a sales-led motion, security reviews, higher price, longer cycles; a SMB/prosumer ICP means self-serve, low price, high volume)
6. What triggers them to look for a solution (new hire, growth, regulation, a failure, a season)?
7. Where do they hang out (communities, events, newsletters, LinkedIn, Slack groups, trade associations)?
8. ★ Can you name 10 real people or companies that fit? How would you reach them this week?
9. What does a *bad-fit* customer look like that you should avoid early?
10. Why are *you* the right founder to sell to this ICP (domain, network, unfair advantage)?

**Gate:** the ICP is narrow enough to build a list of 50 real names, and you can state how it shapes product, pricing, and sales.

## 3 · Product: v0 → v1 → v10 (9)

**v0: prove it (days to weeks)**
1. ★ What's the smallest thing you can put in front of your ICP to prove they want this? Could it be manual, concierge, a landing page, or no-code?
2. ★ Which single assumption does v0 test, and what result means "go"? (e.g. "5 of 20 ICP pay $X" or "3 of 10 use it weekly")

**v1: first real product (weeks to months)**
3. ★ What does v1 do, end to end, for the ICP? What's the one "aha" moment?
4. ★ What is explicitly *not* in v1?
5. What makes v1 10× better than today's workaround (not just slightly better)?
6. What's the exit criterion for v1 (e.g. 10 paying customers, 40% weekly retention, $10K MRR)?

**v10: the vision (3–5+ years)**
7. ★ What does the product look like in 5 years: platform, new customer segments, new products, new geographies?
8. What's the moat at v10 (data, network effects, switching costs, distribution, brand)?
9. What's the path from v1 to v10? Name the 2–3 big steps in between (e.g. v2 adds X, v5 expands to Y).

**Gate:** Is v0 really the smallest test? Is v1 focused on one ICP and one problem? Does v10 justify the ambition?

## 4 · Market Size: TAM / SAM / SOM (7)

1. ★ How many companies or people match your ICP in your first market? (Source or estimate method?)
2. ★ What would one customer pay per year?
3. ★ **SAM** = ICP accounts you can actually reach with v1 × annual price.
4. **TAM** = everyone who could buy the v10 product (all segments and geographies) × annual price.
5. **SOM** = realistic share in 3–5 years given your channels and team. What % and why?
6. What's the beachhead: the smallest segment you can dominate first, and what is next after it?
7. Who are the competitors and alternatives, how big are they, and why will customers pick you?

→ Show the bottom-up math as a table. Sanity-check with web search when available and cite sources. Flag whether the TAM supports a venture-scale company (roughly $1B+ TAM and a credible $100M ARR path) or a strong bootstrapped business.

## 5 · Build Plan (7)

1. ★ Who builds it? What's the team today, and what skills are missing (engineering, design, sales, domain)?
2. ★ How much time are you putting in (full-time or part-time), and what's your runway in months?
3. ★ What's the riskiest technical assumption, and how do you de-risk it first?
4. Build vs buy vs no-code: what can you avoid building for v0 and v1?
5. ★ What's the timeline and budget for v0 → v1? Give milestones with dates.
6. What's the first hire, and when (what trigger or milestone)?
7. What legal and ops basics are needed (incorporation, co-founder equity and vesting, IP assignment, compliance like SOC 2, HIPAA, or PCI)?

## 6 · Sales & Go-to-Market (8)

1. ★ How will you get your first 10 customers? Give names and channels.
2. ★ Which one channel do you focus on first (founder-led outbound, warm intros, community, content/SEO, partnerships, marketplaces, paid, product-led)?
3. ★ What's the sales motion: self-serve, sales-assisted, or enterprise? How long is the sales cycle?
4. What's the one message or hook that makes your ICP reply? Write the first cold message together.
5. What objections will you hear, and how do you answer them?
6. How do customers go from signup to value (onboarding and activation)?
7. What's the path from 10 → 100 → 1,000 customers? When does the founder stop being the salesperson?
8. Which metrics tell you GTM is working (reply rate, demos booked, conversion, activation, churn)?

## 7 · Monetization (8)

1. ★ Who pays: the user, their company, a third party, or advertisers?
2. ★ What's the model: subscription, usage-based, per-seat, transaction fee or take rate, one-time, freemium?
3. ★ What price will you test, and why? (value delivered, ICP budget, competitor prices)
4. What are the pricing tiers or packaging, and what's the upgrade path?
5. What does it cost to acquire a customer (CAC) by channel, even roughly?
6. What's the lifetime value (LTV)? Is LTV:CAC ≥ 3, and is CAC payback < 12–18 months?
7. What are the costs to deliver (infra, support, COGS), and what's the gross margin?
8. ★ How many paying customers do you need for ramen profitability, $1M ARR, and $10M ARR?

## 8 · Fundraising, only if needed (8)

1. ★ Do you *need* to raise? What would you do if you never raised a dollar?
2. ★ Given the TAM and margins, which path fits: bootstrap, revenue-funded, angels or accelerator (e.g. YC), or VC?
3. If raising: ★ what milestone does the money buy (e.g. "v1 + $1M ARR in 18 months")?
4. How much, for how many months of runway, and at what stage (pre-seed, seed, Series A)?
5. What instrument (SAFE, priced round) and what dilution is acceptable?
6. Who are the target investors (angels in the space, specialist funds)? Who can make warm intros?
7. What traction will investors expect at this stage, and do you have it?
8. What's the story in one line: ICP + problem + v10 vision + TAM + traction?

---

## Wrap-up

1. What are the top 3 things you will do in the next 7 days?
2. What would make you pivot or kill this idea? Set the trigger now.
